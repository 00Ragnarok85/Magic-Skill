# Magic Tournament Rules (MTR) Summary

Official Tournament Policies and Deck Compliance Requirements.

---

## 1. Deck Checks & Decklists (MTR 3.11, 3.12)

- In Competitive and Professional Rules Enforcement Level (REL) events:
  - Players must submit an accurate **Decklist** prior to Round 1.
  - The decklist must contain:
    1. Player's full name and DCI / Wizards Account ID.
    2. Format of the event.
    3. Main deck cards with exact quantities and full English names.
    4. Sideboard cards with exact quantities and full English names.
    5. In Commander/Brawl: Explicitly designated Commander card(s).
  - Any discrepancy between the registered decklist and physical deck cards results in a **Decklist Problem penalty** (Tournament Error — Decklist Problem, usually a Game Loss).

---

## 2. Card Authenticity & Sleeve Policies (MTR 3.3, 3.8)

### Authorized Cards (MTR 3.3)
- Players may use genuine Magic: The Gathering cards with standard card backs or official substitute cards.
- **Silver-Bordered / Acorn Cards (CR 100.7):** Unglued, Unhinged, Unstable silver-bordered cards and acorn-stamped Unfinity cards are NOT legal in sanctioned constructed tournaments.
- **Gold-Bordered Cards:** Collector's Edition and World Championship deck cards have gold borders and non-standard backs; they are NOT tournament legal.
- **Altered Cards (MTR 3.3):** Artistic alterations are acceptable provided they:
  1. Do not make the card marked (thickness/feel).
  2. Do not obscure the mana cost or card name.
  3. Do not contain offensive illustrations.
  4. Are approved by the Head Judge prior to the tournament.

### Sleeves & Marked Cards (MTR 3.8)
- Sleeves must be identical in color, condition, and opacity.
- Highly reflective or holographic sleeves that obscure the card face are prohibited.
- Double-faced cards (DFCs) must either be played in completely opaque sleeves or represented by official Substitute Cards in the deck.

---

## 3. Shuffling and Randomization (MTR 3.9)

- Decks must be randomized thoroughly before each game.
- A player must present their deck to their opponent for cutting or shuffling.
- Pile shuffling alone is **not** considered sufficient randomization (it may only be used once at the beginning of a game to count cards).
- Players must be able to shuffle their entire deck **unassisted** within a reasonable time limit (normally under 2 minutes). This practically bounds maximum deck size in formats without a hard cap.
